Combat

Every swing in the game runs through one function, and it is short. Your offence divides into the defender's defence to produce a percentage; a hundred-sided die decides whether you connect; if you do, the weapon rolls damage, the defender's armour subtracts from it, two caps trim the result, and the health comes off. Everything else — proficiencies, might, shields, enchantments, factions — is a term inside one of those five steps.

The whole of an attack

chance = bound( attacker.GetOffense × 55 / defender.GetDefense , 10, 95 ) % percent if random(1,100) <= chance: damage = defender.AssessDamage( attacker.GetDamage, attacker.GetDamageType, attacker.GetSpellType ) else: the defender's parry, block or dodge is credited and reported

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler.kod:319 TryAttack; EQUAL_CHANCE_HIT = 55 at :19

Two things are worth reading off that immediately. First, the floor is 10% and the ceiling is 95%: no amount of skill makes you untouchable, and nothing you fight is ever truly safe to stand next to. Second, evenly matched combatants hit each other 55% of the time — that constant is what the whole scale is calibrated against.

Offence: your chance to land it

offence = stroke_ability × 3 + proficiency × 2 + aim × 4 + base_max_health × 3 / 2 then the weapon's own hit modifier is applied then every attack modifier (spells, gauntlets, rings) then the faction hitroll bonus then × (100 + piFlags3) / 100 bounded to 1…1000 if using a ranged weapon with no line of sight: offence = offence / 2

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:4227 GetOffense

Unarmed, the stroke term is your punch ability and the proficiency term is brawling.

Note what carries the most weight: aim, at ×4, and then your maximum health at ×1.5, which is why a character who has been advancing hits harder to hit things even without touching a weapon skill. The stroke matters more than the proficiency, 3 to 2.

Defence: your chance to avoid it

defence = parry_ability × 2 + block_ability × 1 + dodge_ability × 3 + agility × 4 + base_max_health × 3 / 2 then every worn defence modifier's ModifyDefensePower (armour, ±) then the faction defence bonus then × (100 + piFlags3) / 100 bounded to 1…1000

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:4294 GetDefense

Parry is zero unless you have a weapon in hand. Block is zero unless you have a shield. Dodge always applies. So dodge is the most valuable of the three — it is weighted highest at ×3 and it costs no equipment — and agility is the most valuable attribute for staying alive, at ×4.

Some strokes cannot be parried, blocked or dodged. The stroke object is asked first (CanParry, CanBlock, CanDodge), and a stroke that refuses zeroes that term entirely for that attack.

Damage: what a hit takes off

The weapon rolls first, the stroke scales it, and then the wielder's might and proficiency are added:

base = random(min, max) by weapon type % slash 5–11, bludgeon 4–8, thrust 3–8 + quality % low −1, normal 0, high +1, nerudite +1 + weapon attribute modifiers % enchantments + piDamageBonus stroke: d = base × viDamage_factor / 100 % 80 for the default Slash stroke factors: damage = (proficiency + 1) × viMaxProficiencyDamage / 100 + d × (100 + bound(might − 25, 0, 40)) / 100 bounded to at least 1

kod/object/item/passitem/weapon.kod:241 GetBaseDamage, :280 GetDamage · kod/object/passive/skill/stroke.kod:201 FindDamage, :332 DamageFactors

Might below 26 buys nothing. The multiplier is bound(might − 25, 0, 40), so every point of might from 1 to 25 is dead weight, and every point above 65 is too. A character with might 25 hits exactly as hard as one with might 1.

Proficiency's contribution is smaller than it looks: at the default viMaxProficiencyDamage = 5, a perfect 99 proficiency adds 5 damage. It earns its keep in the offence calculation, not here.

What the defender does with it

for each worn defence modifier: damage = damage − bound(random(flat/3, flat), 0, damage − 1) the item takes wear on a 50% roll resistance = largest matching resistance + worst matching weakness % clipped to ±100 damage = damage × (100 − resistance) / 100 % resistance > 0 = damage × (−100 + resistance) / −100 % resistance < 0 damage = damage + attack-modifier bonuses % added AFTER resistance if damage ≤ 0: damage = 1 unless health is over twice maximum, or you are an outlaw or murderer: damage = bound(damage, $, (base_max_health + 2) / 3) % the one-third cap damage = bound(damage, $, 30) % MAX_DAMAGE_PER_HIT if attacker and defender are enemy faction soldiers: damage = damage × 115 / 100 % applied above both caps

kod/object/item/passitem/defmod.kod:100 ModifyDefenseDamage · kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler.kod:189 ResistanceCheck, :251 GetDamageFromResistance · kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:4556 AssessDamage

Three consequences that change how you play:

  • Nothing can take more than 30 health from you in one blow, ever, and nothing can take more than a third of your maximum. Against a very heavy hitter, more maximum health is worth less than it looks; against a swarm, it is worth exactly what it looks.
  • Outlaws and murderers lose the one-third cap. Marking yourself makes every blow against you potentially three times worse.
  • Armour is subtracted before resistance and bonuses are added after, so an enchanted weapon's bonus damage is never scaled down by the target's armour.
Armour does not stop spells. The flat reduction is zeroed when the attack carries a spell type and no weapon type, and cut to two thirds when it carries both. Only typed resistances reach magic. kod/object/item/passitem/defmod.kod:110–122

Fighting monsters

A creature has no attack table. Its offence, defence, health and damage all come out of two class variables:

offence = defence = 3 × level + 60 × difficulty bounded to 1…1500 health = Fuzzy(level) for level < 40 = Fuzzy(level × 120 / 100) for level ≥ 40 damage per hit = Fuzzy(level / random(10, 15)) dodge = difficulty × 10 Fuzzy(n) = n − n/4 + random(0, n/2) i.e. 75%…125% of n

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/monster.kod:1433, :1455, :1488, :4079, :4263

Difficulty is weighted twenty times more heavily than level in a creature's offence and defence. A high-level, zero-difficulty creature is a sack of health that swings hard and is easy to hit; a mid-level, high-difficulty creature is a wall. The bestiary gives both numbers, and the resulting hit percentages, for every creature.

A worked example

A mid-game character — 130 maximum health, dodge 50, parry 40, no shield, agility 40, stroke 50, proficiency 50, aim 40 — with a long sword and might 40, against a cave orc (level 80, difficulty 7):

ValueWhere it comes from
Your offence60550×3 + 50×2 + 40×4 + 130×3/2
Your defence58540×2 + 0 + 50×3 + 40×4 + 130×3/2
Orc's offence and defence6603×80 + 60×7
Your chance to hit it50%bound(605 × 55 / 660, 10, 95)
Its chance to hit you62%bound(660 × 55 / 585, 10, 95)
Your damage per landed hit3–9random(3,8) × 80/100, × (100+15)/100, + 2
Its damage per landed hit4–10Fuzzy(80 / random(10,15))
Its health72–120Fuzzy(80 × 120/100)

That is a fight you lose. You need roughly 32 landed hits at a 50% rate — 64 swings — while it needs 19 landed hits at 62% — 31 swings. The fix is not more health; it is armour that resists what it swings, a slashing weapon instead of a thrusting one, and dodge.

Timing and rate limits

You may attack or cast once per second, and the server discards anything past five requests per second without telling you. There is no error message for either; the request simply never happened.
