Vigor

Vigor is the stamina bar: a number from 1 to 200 that you spend by moving, swinging and casting, and recover by resting and eating. It is not a resource you spend on anything directly — nothing costs "10 vigor" the way a spell costs mana. Instead it is a multiplier sitting under both regeneration formulas, and it is the single largest lever either of them has. Resting stops at 80. Only food goes past that, and the gap between 80 and 200 is worth roughly three times your health regeneration.

Vigor is stored as two numbers. piVigor is the visible 1–200 value; piExertion is a hidden accumulator in units of 10−4 vigor, so 10 000 exertion = 1 vigor point. Everything that costs or restores vigor writes to the accumulator, and vigor only actually moves once the accumulator passes MIN_VIGOR_CHANGE = 20 000 in either direction (kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:54, :1266–1283). So vigor changes in lumps of two or three points at a time, never one — and small costs are banked, not discarded.

Two constants in this file are dead. ATTACK_EXERTION = 5000 ("effective vigor debit per attack") and REST_TIME = 2500 ("number of ms required to gain one vigor point") are declared at player.kod:57 and :60 and are never read anywhere in the tree. The real per-attack cost is the stroke's own viSkillExertion, and the real rest interval is computed from Stamina. The header comment's claim that a full rest takes 200 seconds (player.kod:37–41) is arithmetic on those dead constants — 80 × 2500 / 1000 — and happens to be right only for a Stamina-1 character.

What vigor actually does

Five things read vigor. Two of them are the regeneration formulas, and they read it very differently.

1. Health regeneration — quadratic

t = ((200 - Vigor) * (200 - Vigor)) / 6 + 1000 % milliseconds per hit point, before Stamina and max health

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:5617

Vigor enters as the square of the shortfall from 200, so its value accelerates: the last quarter of the bar is worth more than the first three quarters put together.

2. Mana regeneration — a straight divisor

t = t * 200 / bound(piVigor, 1, $)

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:5657

Here vigor is exactly linear and there is no floor worth speaking of: Vigor 200 regenerates mana precisely twice as fast as Vigor 100, at every pool size. See Channeling and Mana.

The two formulas disagree about where vigor stops mattering. A Stamina-50 character with 100 maximum health is already pinned at the 1000 ms health floor by Vigor 140 — past that, more vigor buys no health at all. Mana keeps scaling to 200 and beyond. If you are a caster, the top of the vigor bar is still worth eating for; if you are a Stamina-heavy fighter, it is not.

3. Faren spell power

iSecondaryBonus = GetVigor() / 20 % then bound(iSecondaryBonus, 0, 10)

kod/object/passive/spell.kod:2169, :2203

Every Faren spell gets up to +10 spell power from vigor alone — the school "rewards the vigorous warrior", as the comment puts it. Vigor 200 gives the full +10; Vigor 100 gives +5. For comparison, Kraanan's equivalent secondary bonus is your health fraction and Jala's is maxHealth/12. That is a real difference to a Faren caster: +5 spell power moves Mana Focus, Mystic touch and every elemental attack spell.

4. The gate on running

if speed > USER_WALKING_SPEED AND GetVigor() < VIGOR_RUN_THRESHOLD: the server snaps you back to where you were and logs a cheat warning

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player/user.kod:2958–2975; constants USER_WALKING_SPEED = 18 and VIGOR_RUN_THRESHOLD = 10 at :46 and :54

Below 10 vigor you cannot run. The client is supposed to enforce this; the server treats a running move packet from an exhausted player as a possible speedhack, refuses the move and writes an ALERT! line to the log.

Moving

Running is exactly four times as expensive as walking, because the cost is quadratic in speed and running doubles it. Ten minutes of running is 108 vigor — more than the whole rest-able range. Haste's state is its spell power, 1–99 (kod/object/passive/spell/persench/haste.kod:69–71), so it subtracts up to 99 % of movement exertion and is far and away the cheapest way to cross the world with your bar intact.

Attacking

Every swing pays its stroke's cost through the ordinary skill machinery (kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:4070–4073 → kod/object/passive/skill.kod:252–265).

on a successful skill: AddExertion(1000 * viSkillExertion) on a failed skill: AddExertion((1000 * viSkillExertion) / 2)

kod/object/passive/skill.kod:246, :263

Note the multiplier is 1000, not 10 000 — so a skill's viSkillExertion is in tenths of a vigor point, while a spell's viSpellExertion is in whole points.

At 0.2 vigor a swing, sustained melee is cheap — roughly 5 swings per vigor point. Movement, not combat, is what empties the bar.

Thrust and Kick are unfinished, and their exertion values prove it. Thrust declares viskillExertion = 500 and Kick declares 2000 — 50 and 200 vigor per swing. Neither overrides vbCheck_Exertion, so the pre-swing test HasVigor(500) can never pass at a 200-vigor ceiling and the stroke is refused every time. Both are also declared at viSkill_level = 50, far past any real school level, so they cannot be learned in the first place (kod/object/passive/skill/stroke/thrust.kod:37, :40; kod/object/passive/skill/stroke/unarmed/kick.kod:44, :46).

Three near-identical names live in this corner of the tree and only one of them is read: viSkillExertion (skill.kod:60 — the live one), viSkill_Exertion (stroke.kod:59, returned by a GetExertion message that nothing calls) and piSkillExertion (kod/object/passive/skill/assess.kod:50, never read at all).

Casting

on a successful cast: AddExertion(10000 * viSpellExertion) % whole vigor points on a failed cast: AddExertion((10000 * viSpellExertion) / 2)

kod/object/passive/spell.kod:1283 and :1166

A failed spell costs half the vigor, half the mana and no reagents. Immortals and non-player casters pay nothing.

A typical spell costs 10 vigor — fifty times what a sword swing costs. Seven casts of an ordinary spell take a rested character from 80 down to 10, and the eighth is refused. This, not mana, is usually what stops a caster mid-fight, and it is why the food table below matters more to casters than to fighters.

This applies only to costs, never to gains — the test is iExertionAdded > 0 — so it makes you tire more slowly but does not make you rest or eat any faster. It is the only mechanic in the game that reduces vigor spending.

Getting vigor back

Resting — and why it stops at 80

Sit down (the client's UC_REST) and a timer starts that removes exertion until you reach your rest threshold, then stops.

GetRestTime() = 1000 + 30 * (51 - Stamina) % ms between rest ticks (reduced by Invigorate if a bard is singing it) RestTimer(): if piVigor < piVigor_rest_threshold: RestAddExertion(-10000) RestAddExertion(amount): if piVigor > piVigor_rest_threshold: return % never rests you above the threshold if room is ROOM_SANCTUARY: amount = 2 * amount if room is ROOM_TRIPLE_HEAL: amount = 3 * amount piExertion = piExertion + amount if abs(piExertion) > MIN_VIGOR_CHANGE: piVigor = min(piVigor - piExertion/10000, piVigor_rest_threshold) piExertion = 0

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:9984–10045 and :1296–1339

Resting cannot take you above 80. That is the default piVigor_rest_threshold (player.kod:891), and the threshold is itself bounded to 10–100 no matter what modifies it (player.kod:1238). The top 120 points of the vigor bar — where the health formula pays the most — are reachable only by eating.

ROOM_TRIPLE_HEAL does not affect healing. Despite the name, the flag is read in exactly one place in the entire tree — player.kod:1320, inside RestAddExertion — so it triples your vigor recovery and does nothing whatever for health or mana. Exactly one room in the game sets it: the Jasper inn (kod/object/active/holder/room/jasperrm/jasinn.kod:33). Seventeen rooms carry ROOM_SANCTUARY and get the double rate.

Two side effects of the dead band are worth knowing. In a normal room the −10 000 per tick only crosses MIN_VIGOR_CHANGE on the third tick, so vigor arrives in visible jumps of three; in a sanctuary it is four points every two ticks. And RestAddExertion zeroes the accumulator rather than keeping the remainder, so if you sit down with a large positive exertion balance from a fight, the first rest tick swallows it.

While resting you are flagged PFLAG_NO_MOVE, PFLAG_NO_FIGHT and PFLAG_NO_MAGIC (player.kod:1155–1167) — you cannot move, attack or cast, and a spell that finishes its cast time while you are resting is refused (kod/object/passive/spell.kod:739–745, :1793–1798). Resting also breaks a trance immediately (player.kod:9991). Nothing in the server stops you resting in a hostile room, and nothing stands you back up when you are hit; standing is a client command.

Invigorate (Jala 1) multiplies the rest tick by (200 − spellPower)/200, cutting it roughly in half at spell power 99 (kod/object/passive/spell/jala/invigor.kod:61–69). At Stamina 25 that is 1780 ms down to 898 ms. It is room-wide, costs 5 mana plus 1 every 5 s, and needs one diamond and one snack (invigor.kod:35–41, :52–58). It does not raise the threshold — it only gets you to 80 faster.

Eating — the only route past the threshold

EatSomething(nutrition, filling): AddExertion(-10000 * nutrition) % one vigor point per point of nutrition piStomach = piStomach + filling

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:5734–5758; kod/object/item/passitem/numbitem/food.kod:73

This path goes through AddExertion, not RestAddExertion, so it is not threshold-clamped — only NewVigor's ceiling of 200 stops it. You cannot eat past 100 fullness (player.kod:5695–5717), and fullness drains at FOOD_USE_RATE = 12 per 100 seconds, emptying a full stomach in 13.9 minutes (player.kod:51, :1341–1352).

Inky caps are twice as efficient as anything else, and chaos food ignores your stomach entirely. Second Wind

An automatic Fencing skill that fires the moment exertion pushes you below 25 vigor.

trigger: any positive exertion, while ability > 0 and vigor < 25 grant: 40 + Stamina/2 + skill ability vigor points lockout: (600 - 3 * ((100 - Stamina) * ability / 100 + Stamina)) seconds % 5 to 10 minutes during the lockout: rest threshold is pinned at 10 and NO vigor can be gained at all

Second Wind is a bargain that turns into a trap. The grant is applied before the enchantment starts, which is the only reason it works at all — AddExertion and RestAddExertion both return immediately on any negative amount while the enchantment is up, and eating is refused outright with "still feeling a surge of vitality, you have no interest in food or drink right now" (player.kod:1251–1257, :1303–1308; kod/object/passive/skill/secwind.kod:107–120). For the full five to ten minutes your vigor can only go down, and your rest threshold is 10. A maxed character gets 165 vigor up front and then cannot add a single point for five minutes.

The skill also refuses to advance if you have an easy way to trigger it — holding a faction token, wearing a ring of lethargy, or standing in the Underworld all set your advancement chance to zero (secwind.kod:131–139).

The rest threshold

piVigor_rest_threshold is the ceiling on resting and the marker drawn on your vigor bar. It defaults to 80, is reset to 80 on character restart (player.kod:2308), and every write to it is re-bounded to 10–100 — and forced to 10 if Second Wind is active (player.kod:1228–1243).

Reading the vigor bar

AddPacket(1,3, 4,user_stat_vigor, 1,STAT_VALUE, 1,STAT_INTEGER, 4,piVigor, 4,0, 4,200, 4,piVigor_rest_threshold);

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player/user.kod:6834–6839; the client maps these to GRPH_RANGESET / GRPH_POSSET / GRPH_LIMITSET at module/merintr/statnum.c:196–198

Unlike health and mana, the vigor bar's range is honest — 0 to 200, the true full scale. The limit marker is not your maximum, though: it is your rest threshold. That line on the bar is the point at which sitting down stops helping, which is why it usually sits at 80 and why it drops to a sliver while you are carrying a token or riding out a Second Wind.

Quick reference

  1. Eat before you fight, not after. Resting stops at 80; food is the only way to 200, and the health formula pays quadratically for the difference.
  2. Inky caps, then mints. Twice the vigor per point of stomach; mints add mana on top.
  3. Walk in safe areas. Running costs four times as much per second — 10.8 vigor a minute against 2.7.
  4. Casting is the real drain. An ordinary spell costs 10 vigor, fifty sword swings' worth. Melee barely touches the bar.
  5. Stamina buys rest speed. Stamina 50 rests almost 2.5× faster than Stamina 1, and inns double it again.
  6. Do not carry a token if you plan to fight. It pins your rest threshold at 10 and blocks Second Wind advancement.
  7. Second Wind is a one-shot. It hands a maxed character 165 vigor, then locks out every source of vigor — including food — for five to ten minutes.
