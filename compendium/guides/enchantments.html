Enchantments and Status Effects

Everything that is temporarily true about a character — blessed, hasted, poisoned, invisible, demented — is an enchantment: a spell object attached to you with a duration and a state, which the combat code consults on every swing. There are 35 personal enchantments, 14 that sit on a room, 4 sicknesses and 16 attributes that live on a weapon. Almost every duration scales with the caster's spell power, and this page gives all of them.

How an enchantment works

CastSpell: if already enchanted and the server permits recasting: remove the old one first StartEnchantment( what = the spell object, state = InitializeEnchantmentState(spell power, caster, target), time = GetDuration(spell power), lastcall, addicon ) while it is on you: the spell object is in plAttack_modifiers or plDefense_modifiers, and combat calls ModifyHitRoll / ModifyDamage / ModifyDefensePower / ModifyDefenseDamage on it EndEnchantment: a message, and the modifier is removed

kod/object/passive/spell/persench.kod:151 CastSpell, :199 EndEnchantment, :209 InitializeEnchantmentState

The state is the important part. Most enchantments store the spell power they were cast at, so the strength of the effect is fixed at cast time and does not change if the caster later gets stronger or dies. An enchantment is a snapshot.

The framework is the same one armour uses. A worn defence modifier and a defensive spell both end up in plDefense_modifiers and are both asked to modify incoming damage in the same loop — which is why the armour page's order of operations applies to spells too.

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:4556 AssessDamage, :4265 (attack modifiers)

Recasting replaces, it does not stack. If the server allows self-recasting, the existing enchantment is removed before the new one is applied — so recasting at higher spell power is an upgrade, and recasting at lower power is a downgrade. There is no way to have two of the same enchantment.

Three patterns are worth pulling out of that table:

  • Some durations barely scale and some scale enormously. Invisibility goes from 60 to 218 — more than triple. Resist poison does not scale at all: 900 whatever you do.
  • Four enchantments are randomised on top — bless, umbrella, detect evil and detect good all roll random(D/2, D), so half the time you get half the duration you were expecting.
  • Touch attacks are counted in hits, not seconds. random(P/3, P/2) charges, so a weak caster gets six swings of acid touch and a strong one gets forty. See Touch Spell.

Dementia is the most dangerous status effect a caster can take, because it is applied to spell power — which sets success chance, mana cost, casting speed and effect strength all at once. One dementia degrades four things simultaneously.

Item attributes: enchantments that live on gear

Thirty-one item attributes exist, sixteen of them specific to weapons. They are not spells — they are properties rolled onto an item, most often by the treasure system, and they show up in the item's name: "enchanted %s", "%s of the master", "%s of the defender", "%s of the just", "glowing %s", "cursed %s", "transcendant %s", "well-crafted %s", and twenty-three variants that simply read "special %s".

weapon attributes may only ADD or SUBTRACT damage — never multiply for each attribute on the weapon: damage = attribute.ModifyDamage(damage, wielder, target) then damage = damage + piDamageBonus

kod/object/item/passitem/weapon.kod:280 GetDamage — the "no multipliers" rule is stated in the source comment

Where they come from: each treasure type carries a difficulty seed and a chance out of 400. Before the drop table is consulted at all, a player-landed kill rolls against that chance for an item-attributed item instead of an ordinary one.

kod/object/passive/trestype.kod:270

Attributes also change what an item is worth: GetValue runs every attribute's AdjustPrice over the condition-adjusted value. A cursed item is worth less; an enchanted one more.

kod/object/item.kod:437–443

Practical advice

  • Cast your buffs at your best. Duration and strength are both snapshotted at cast time from your spell power — which means casting Qor buffs at midnight, Kraanan buffs in a crowd and Riija buffs with a full pack all produce materially longer, stronger effects. See Spells and Spellcasting.
  • Recast to upgrade, never to stack. The old enchantment is removed first.
  • Resistances are the answer to spell damage, because armour is not — the flat reduction is zeroed against pure magic. Match the resistance to what you are about to fight; the bestiary lists each creature's attack type.
  • Watch for dementia before a fight you need magic to win. It attacks the one number everything else derives from.
  • Touch attacks are charges, not time. They will not expire while you wait, and a high-power cast gives you forty swings.
