Advancing Your Character

There are three separate progressions in Meridian 59 and they do not share a budget. Your maximum health grows by killing things harder than you are. Your skills and spells grow by using them, on a clock that caps how fast that can happen. Your attributes never grow at all — they are fixed the moment the character is made, and one of them silently sets the ceiling on the first progression. That last fact is the single most consequential thing on this page.

Stamina is the decision you cannot take back. Your maximum health can never exceed 101 + stamina, and stamina also divides into the rate at which health is gained — so a low-stamina character both climbs more slowly and stops sooner. There is no in-game way to raise it. See Attributes before you make a character, not after.

Maximum health: what a kill is worth

A kill earns you nothing at all unless you damaged the creature and it was your current target. Switching targets mid-fight throws away the credit for the old one, because each new attack resets the flags.

Given that, the kill is scored:

Points accumulate in a hidden counter. When a kill qualifies for a roll, the server rolls against that counter:

index = base_max_health × (100 − stamina) / 100 highmark = (index + 1) × index roll = random(1, highmark) threshold = accumulated_points + bound((creature_level − base_max_health) / 5, 0, 10) if roll < threshold AND base_max_health < (101 + stamina): base_max_health += 1 accumulated_points = −(base_max_health / 2) if base_max_health > 30: accumulated_points −= (50 − stamina) / 2 you are healed to full and fed

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:7823–7854, GetHighMark at :7896

Three things fall out of that, and they matter more than the formula does.

  • Fighting things far above you is worth more than fighting more things. The threshold gets a bonus of up to +10 for the gap between the creature's level and your health, and that bonus is added on every roll.
  • Gaining a point costs you points. The counter is reset to a negative number after each gain — deeper the higher your health and the lower your stamina — so each health point is more expensive than the last.
  • Stamina compounds twice. It raises the ceiling and it shrinks the highmark you are rolling under.

How many kills is that, actually

Accumulating 3 points per qualifying kill against a highmark H, the expected number of kills to gain one health point is roughly √(2H/3). At 50 maximum health:

The high-stamina character reaches a ceiling 60 points higher and gets there roughly three times faster. Nothing else in character creation is worth this much.

A character created through the administrative socket has zero in every attribute — displayed as 1, because the accessors clamp the raw value upward — and can therefore never exceed 102 maximum health. If your character's ceiling looks wrong, this is why, and the only fix is a new character.

Where a kill earns nothing at all

No advancement happens in a room flagged ROOM_SAFE_DEATH, nor in an arena unless that arena has real death switched on, nor on any creature that refuses to be advanced upon — revenants and illusions, the two things a player can conjure at will.

Skills and spells: ability from use

Every skill and spell carries an ability from 0 to 99. It is both how well the thing works and how likely it is to work at all, and it moves only when you use it. Each use runs two gates.

initial gate: chance = viChance_to_Increase + (viChance_to_Increase × intellect) / 100 + faction learning bonus random(1,100) must come in at or under it second gate: chance = the spell or skill's own secondary chance, adjusted by the room, then bound(chance, 1 + requisite_stat/10, 99) random(0,100) must come in under it both passed -> ability += 1

kod/object/passive/spell.kod:1604 ImproveAbility, :1693 GetInitialChance, :1711 GetSecondaryChance

Intellect adds up to 50% to the first gate. The relevant attribute for the spell's school sets a floor under the second, so a caster with the right attribute never has a hopeless chance even at high ability.

The cap that stops you grinding

You may gain at most 10 improvements across all your skills and spells combined before a timer resets the counter. The timer is set to a random random(900000, 1320000) milliseconds — 15 to 22 minutes.

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:66–68, :7629 AddAdvancementPoints, :7647 CheckAdvancementPoints

Once you have hit the cap, further practice does nothing at all until the window rolls over. There is no message when you hit it, only an occasional hint. The practical consequence: spread your practice. Ten improvements is ten, whether they land on one spell or on eight, and a session that alternates between fighting, casting and travelling extracts more than one that grinds a single ability.

Atrophy: the number goes down

When the advancement timer fires, anything you did not use in that window is a candidate to lose ability. The chance and the amount both depend on how much you learned and on your intellect:

highmark = advancement_points + ((50 − bound(intellect, 1, 50)) × advancement_points) / 20 for each unused spell or skill: if random(1, 200) < highmark and its ability > 2: lose 1 point if you used something else in the same school this window lose 2 points otherwise

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:7196 ActivateSpellAtrophy, :7568 ActivateSkillAtrophy, :7680 AdvancementTimer

Read that carefully, because it is counter-intuitive: the more you learned this window, the more everything else decays. A hard grinding session actively costs you the abilities you neglected during it. Low intellect makes it worse — up to two and a half times worse at intellect 1. And staying inside one school halves the loss, which is a real argument for specialising.

Abilities at 2 or below never atrophy, so nothing is ever lost completely. And atrophy only runs when the server has it enabled.

Attributes: chosen once, forever

Nothing in the game raises a base attribute. Every accessor reads the stored value plus a temporary modifier from spells or items and clamps the result; there is no write path outside character creation and administrative commands.

Might below 25 does literally nothing — the bonus is bound(might − 25, 0, 40), so points 1 through 25 buy zero damage, and points above 65 buy zero more. If you are building a fighter, either clear 25 or do not spend there at all. See Attributes for the full treatment.

A plan that works

Before you play: build the character

  • Decide the ceiling first. Stamina is health, permanently. Anything you leave out of stamina you are choosing never to have.
  • Fighters: stamina, then agility and aim, then might — but only if you can reach 26 or more, because below that it is wasted.
  • Casters: stamina, then intellect and mysticism. Intellect pays twice, once on learning speed and once on not losing what you learned.
  • Pick your school before you pick your karma. Qor and Shal'ille pull in opposite directions and you cannot have both.

The first hours

  • Your max health starts low, which means almost everything qualifies as "above your level" and almost every kill counts. This is the fastest the health progression will ever be — and you get +1 on every kill until you pass 30. Use it.
  • Buy the cheap level-1 abilities early; they improve from use, so the sooner you own them the sooner the ability starts moving. See who teaches what.
  • Get some armour, but read Armour first: the heavy pieces carry a negative defence bonus, and early on being hit less is worth more than being hit softly.

Every session after that

  • Fight things that frighten you. A creature below your level is worth one point and no roll. A creature above it is worth three points, a roll, and up to +10 on that roll. Sort the bestiary by how likely it is to hit you and work down the list.
  • Land the killing blow and take a hit doing it. Both are required for the full three points, and the second one is easy to forget if you are a caster.
  • Do not switch targets. The kill only counts for the creature you were last attacking.
  • Rotate your practice. Ten improvements per 15–22 minutes is the ceiling regardless. Spend it across several abilities rather than exhausting it on one.
  • Touch everything you care about once per window, or it decays — and it decays harder precisely on the sessions where you learned the most.
  • Record your abilities at the start of a session and compare at the end. It is the only progress signal the game gives for skills and spells, and the deltas are too small to remember.
