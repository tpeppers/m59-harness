Shrines

A shrine is a stone object in a room that is dedicated to one of the six gods, and while it is dedicated it adds a flat bonus to the spell power of every caster of that god in the world — not just the person who dedicated it. There are three of them, each behind something unpleasant, and any player can take one from another god by walking up and leaving a different offering. That is the whole design: a small permanent buff to a whole faction, held in a place that is deliberately hard to reach and impossible to hold.

What the bonus actually is

Every shrine declares piShrine_power = 6. The server keeps a running total per school in plShrine_Powers, rebuilt from scratch whenever any shrine changes hands, and a spell being cast adds its school's total after every other modifier has been applied.

RecomputeShrineTotals() plShrine_Powers = [0,0,0,0,0,0] % one slot per school for each shrine: plShrine_Powers[allegiance] += GetPower() % 6 per shrine GetShrineBonus(school) 0 if school is outside SS_SHALILLE(1) … SS_JALA(6) 5 if plShrine_Powers has never been built plShrine_Powers[school] otherwise % and in the spell itself: iSpellPower = iSpellPower + iShrines + iItemModifier

kod/util/system.kod:2795 RecomputeShrineTotals, :2827 GetShrineBonus; kod/object/passive/spell.kod:2221–2225; kod/object/passive/shrine.kod:48 piShrine_power

Two things about that read backwards. The bonus is added last, after all the ordinary scaling, so it is a flat addition rather than a percentage and is worth proportionally more to a weak caster than a strong one. And the fallback when the table has never been built is 5, not 0 — a freshly started server gives every school a small bonus until the first recompute, so a measurement taken early is not a measurement of anybody's shrines.

How a shrine changes hands

You do not talk to a shrine or use it. You drop an offering next to it. The room posts a check on anything that lands, and if the item is within one square of the shrine and is an Offering, the shrine takes it.

  • GotOffering tests IsClass(what,&Offering). Anything else gets "The shrine finds nothing to its liking."
  • MakeOffering sets piAllegiance to the offering's own god, calls RecomputeShrineTotals, and deletes the offering — the gods eat it.
  • An offering is a plain PassiveItem: average value 1000, weight 25, bulk 30, carrying a vrAllegiance that says which god it belongs to.

kod/object/passive/shrine.kod:82 GotOffering, :101 MakeOffering; kod/object/item/passitem/offering.kod; kod/util/system.kod ShrineCheckItem (radius test is distance² < radius², called with radius 1)

Dedicating a shrine is announced to every player logged on. Not to the room, not to the guild — TellServer loops over GetUsersLoggedOn and sends each of them a line and a sound chosen by the god: a triumphant call for Kraanan, a chill up the spine for Qor, mischievous noises for Riija. There is no quiet way to do this, which is the point of it being contested.

The numbering is 1, 3, 5 with gaps. Two more identifiers exist in the range and nothing in the room tree creates a shrine with them, so either two shrines were planned and never built or two were removed — a constant that exists is not an object that stands somewhere, and the source cannot tell the difference.

The Forest Shrine maze, in detail

This is the one worth explaining, because it is the one that kills. Room 555 is a field of acidic fog with a single safe row through it. The room picks that row at random when the puzzle initialises, and it moves as you cross.

Standing at the entrance is an NPC, Lady Phoenix. You ask her for protection; she registers you as the runner and then tells you, one step at a time, which row is currently safe. Only one runner is allowed at a time — a second is told "A mysterious force prevents you from entering the gas field." and refused.

Step onto a wrong square and the room punishes you:

PunishPlayer(who) damage = GetBaseMaxHealth(who) / 3 % as ATCK_SPELL_ACID if AssessDamage(...) kills them: killed(who) % outright, not "reduced to 0" "The acid steam melts the flesh from your bones." otherwise: teleport them back to row 9, col 3 % the entrance InitPuzzle() % and the safe row is re-rolled

kod/object/active/holder/room/monsroom/e5.kod:452 PunishPlayer, :42 and :44 for the two messages

A third of base max health per wrong step means three mistakes kill anyone, from full health, regardless of level — armour does not enter into it and neither does how tough the character is, because the damage is computed from their own maximum. And being bounced back to the entrance re-rolls the puzzle, so a failed attempt does not leave you with partial knowledge of the route.

This room is a hard block for bots in this repository. Nothing about the safe row is visible to a router: it is random, it moves, and the only way to learn it is to hold a conversation with an NPC. A character that has not been taught the protocol is not taking a risk in there, it is walking a fixed number of steps to its death — which is how the fleet lost Statler. Room 555 is in NEVER_ENTER in tools/m59-map.mjs: it is removed from the routing graph on every pass including the permissive fallback, so an errand that would need to cross it fails and says so. A character already standing in it can always walk out, and an operator can still send someone deliberately by passing allowHazardDestination.

So why would anyone run it?

Not for loot — there is none. The shrine pays nothing to the person who reaches it. What it buys is +6 spell power for every caster of your god on the server, for as long as it stays yours, plus a server-wide announcement that it happened.

That makes shrines a faction objective rather than a personal one, and the only kind of persistent territory in the game that is contested by errand rather than by combat. Holding one is not possible: there is no lock, no cooldown and no ownership check on MakeOffering. Anyone who can reach the shrine with an offering of their own takes it, immediately, and the whole world is told. The maze is not a difficulty gate protecting a prize — it is the cost of flipping it, paid again by every side, every time.
