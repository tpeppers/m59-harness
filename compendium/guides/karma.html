Karma

Karma is one number between −100 and +100, and it is the only character statistic that moves as a consequence of what you do rather than how much you practise. It gates two of the six schools of magic against each other — a deep Qor caster and a deep Shal'ille caster cannot be the same character — and it moves mostly by killing. What you fight is what you become.

Where the number lives

Karma is stored as piKarma in hundredths of a karma point and clamped to ±10000, so the scale you actually see runs −100 to +100. Everything a player reads is piKarma / 100.

GetKarma() = piKarma / 100 % what every rule below compares against NewKarma() clamps piKarma to −10000 … +10000 AddKarma(n) piKarma += n % n is in hundredths GainKarma(n) piKarma += 100 × n % n is in whole karma points LoseKarma(n) piKarma −= 100 × n

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:822, :6432 GetKarma, :6440 AddKarma, :6465 NewKarma

The hundredths matter: a single monster kill is capped at 150 hundredths, that is 1.5 karma points. Moving from neutral to the −60 a level-6 Qor spell demands is a minimum of forty kills, and in practice many more, because the change per kill shrinks as you approach the karma of what you are killing.

What a kill does

A kill is treated as an act whose karma is the negative of the victim's. Killing something with +100 karma is a −100 act; killing something with −100 karma is a +100 act.

act = −(victim_karma) base = your_karma − act change = −( base³ / 2500 + 5 × base ) / (11 − swing) % hundredths of a point swing = bound(swing, 1, 10) % 10 swings hardest monsters: change = bound(change, −150, +150)

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:6491 CalculateKarmaChangeFromAct, :6524 CalculateKarmaChangeFromKill

The cubic term is what makes this interesting. The further your karma is from the act, the faster you move — but the change is capped, so the practical effect is a soft ceiling that pulls you toward the karma of whatever you habitually kill and then stops. Killing evil creatures makes you good, quickly at first and then slowly, and it cannot make you better than the mirror of what you are killing.

That last row is the one people misread. If you are at +40 karma and you do a +20 act, you get nothing — the rule only fires when a good character does something bad, or a bad character does something good. Karma is easy to lose and hard to deepen.

The newbie-region exemption exists for a specific reason, stated in the source: without it, a new character killing a negative-karma monster could accidentally push their karma past the gate on a spell they already own and render it uncastable.

The gate on magic

Karma is not stored on a spell. It is derived from the spell's school and level:

Qor requires karma ≤ level × −10 % you must be that evil Shal'ille requires karma ≥ level × +10 % you must be that good Kraanan, Faren, Riija, Jala: no requirement

The gate is checked at cast time, not at learning time. You can learn a Qor spell, drift positive, and discover the spell you paid for no longer works. Nothing is refunded and nothing is forgotten — it simply refuses.

The strategic consequence is the whole shape of a caster's career: the two polar schools are mutually exclusive at every level, and the four neutral schools are available to anyone. A character who wants deep Qor must live at −60 or below, which means habitually killing good creatures, which means the town guards and most merchants will treat them accordingly.

What to kill, and which way it moves you

A creature's viKarma is its alignment. Killing it moves you the opposite way. Here is the distribution across the bestiary:

Most of the world is neutral, which means most fighting does not move your karma at all. Deliberately shifting alignment means seeking out the minority of creatures that carry a value — sort the bestiary by its karma column.

Other things that move it

  • Killing a player — the same formula with a different swing factor, and no ±150 cap. Player kills move karma far harder than monster kills, and killing an innocent also sets the murderer flag.
  • Casting — several Shal'ille spells award karma for helping. Minor heal says so in its own description: healing another good soul is rewarded.
  • The karma potion — an item that moves the number directly.
  • Faction and political acts — see Factions.

What else notices your karma

  • Creatures with MOB_KARMA_AG will not attack players of like karma. An evil character can walk past evil monsters that would savage a good one. This is a real defensive property, not flavour. kod/include/blakston.khd:1403
  • Merchants flagged MOB_LAWFUL refuse to do business publicly with murderers. kod/include/blakston.khd:1407
  • Spell success against a target scales with the karma gap between you: chance += abs(target_karma − your_karma) / 20. kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/player.kod:5104

If you are building a character

Decide the polarity before you spend a single shilling on a spell.

  • Neutral is the default and costs nothing. Kraanan, Faren, Riija and Jala are all fully available, and you can fight anything without consequence.
  • Shal'ille requires living at +60 or above for its deepest spells, sustained by killing evil creatures — which is most of what a conventional adventurer fights anyway. It is the cheap polarity to hold.
  • Qor requires −60 or below, sustained by killing good creatures and players. It is the expensive polarity: it costs you the goodwill of lawful merchants and makes you a target, and the creatures you must hunt are rarer.
