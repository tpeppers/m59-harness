Skills

22 skills, in three families that behave completely differently: strokes decide how your weapon swings, proficiencies decide how well you handle a class of weapon, and skills proper are things you invoke. All three share one number — an ability from 0 to 99 that rises with use and falls with neglect.

The three families

Stroke
A stroke is a way of swinging. You do not invoke it; the weapon picks one, and its ability decides both how often you connect and how hard. Improving a stroke improves every weapon that uses it.
Proficiency
A proficiency is a weapon class. It contributes to your chance to hit and adds a small flat bonus to damage — (proficiency + 1) × 5 / 100 per swing — and it is what the weapon checks before you can use it well at all.
Skill
A skill proper is something you invoke, or that fires automatically when its condition is met. It costs vigour, it can fail, and its ability is both the success chance and the size of the effect.

What ability is worth

Ability enters combat in three places, and it is worth knowing which, because they are not equally valuable. Your stroke ability is multiplied by 3 in your offence; your proficiency by 2; your aim by 4. Defence weights dodge by 3, parry by 2 and block by 1.

offence = stroke×3 + proficiency×2 + aim×4 + max_health×3/2 defence = parry×2 + block×1 + dodge×3 + agility×4 + max_health×3/2 chance to hit = bound(attacker_offence × 55 / defender_defence, 10, 95) %
