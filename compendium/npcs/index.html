NPCs

82 people you can do business with. There is no NPC class in this game: an innkeeper is a creature with the fighting turned off and a list of goods, which is why each of these links to a bestiary page. What separates them from the ants is a flag.

How this list was chosen

Every Monster subclass whose viAttributes carries at least one of the trading flags, or which puts anything in plFor_Sale, or which is marked as never fighting:

FlagMeaning
MOB_NOFIGHTDoesn't fight
MOB_LISTENResponds to speech
MOB_RECEIVEWill accept items/gifts
MOB_BUYERWill buy items
MOB_SELLERHas items for sale
MOB_BANKERCan be a banker (set Bank ID!!!)
MOB_SMITHCan repair things (NOT IMPLEMENTED)
MOB_TEACHERHas skills or spells for sale
MOB_COND_SELLERHas things that must be asked for
MOB_PERM_QUESTERis a perm questor
MOB_VAULTMANCan be a vaultkeeper (set Vault ID!!!)
MOB_LAWFULWon't publicly do business with murderers

What they charge

Every price a merchant quotes is the item's declared value run through one number, viMerchant_markup, and the two multipliers are asymmetric: 20 points a step when you buy, 10 when you sell. So the round-trip spread grows much faster than either column suggests, and where you shop matters more than what you carry. The Prices column below is sortable and filterable — sort it to put the four bargains at the top.

A band is not an invitation to sell. Skivlat the Tos banker sits in the bargain band and pays nothing at all — bankers take what you hand over and thank you, and the two vaults are storage that sells your goods back at about a shilling. The flags say buys_anything for all of them and it is true and it is not a market. See Money and Merchants for who actually pays.
