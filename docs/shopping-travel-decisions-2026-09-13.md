**Which survival decisions differ, and what they cost — September 13, 2026**

Giving an internal shopping journey a free pass loop would principally add opportunities to cancel movement and reconnect when health is low or falling quickly. It would not simply add more observation, and it would not automatically add useful shelter. Continuing to walk can be the better action when an exit or verified refuge is near; intervention can be the better action when the route is not going to get the character out of danger. The present code does not compare those alternatives directly.

This is a decision analysis of `5a84258` and the accompanying track-shelter repair. It makes no change to shopping scheduling, guard switches, or freeze invalidation. The [execution report](internal-shopping-survival-2026-09-13.md) explains why some calls are delayed, including why “internal versus external” is not a permanent property of a journey. The [offline comparison](reproductions/shopping-survival.mjs) establishes which branches execute; it does not measure their effect on survival.

**The decisions that actually differ.** The following table compares a shopping journey occupying the ordinary pass with otherwise identical travel whose pass loop is available. Conditions refer to executable branches in [Autopilot.passTravelling](../tools/m59-autopilot.mjs), not the older comments surrounding them.

| Decision | Actual trigger and action with a free pass loop | While shopping occupies the pass | Benefit the action seeks | What it gives up or can get wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absolute low health (`flee`, default on) | Below `safety().fleeAt`, with an attackable monster or qualifying player within 2 squares: attempt `playDead`. Successful monster reconnect cancels movement, suspends the destination, and requests recovery. A qualifying player also causes abandonment of the suspended journey, even if the reconnect fails. | This branch is not evaluated until the pass returns. Independent wedge rescue and mover shelter still exist. | Stop an attack before the remaining health is exhausted; this also works when health is already low and no longer falling enough to give a useful rate. | Loses immediate progress toward the exit/refuge. Reconnect and recovery take time. A nearby stranger is classified from flags, distance and fleet membership, not proof that the player attacked. A monster reconnect is conditional on `playDead` accepting and succeeding. |
| Rapid health loss (`fight_back`, default on) | Estimated time to death at or below 10 seconds by default: attempt `playDead`, then cancel and take the journey back even if the reconnect did not succeed. Preserve the destination for monsters; abandon under `travelFleeFrom: anything` or with a qualifying nearby player. | The rate may still be observable, but this decision is not dispatched. | Catch a moving character whose health is collapsing, including cases a stillness detector misses. | The threshold is **not compared with the remaining travel time**, distance to the door, or time to an available refuge. A character one second from safety can still be stopped. The branch does not require a nearby attacker and does not establish what caused the health loss. A failed reconnect can leave a cancelled journey needing recovery. |
| Rearm (`arm`, default off) | If explicitly enabled and `armed()` says no: cancel, preserve the destination, request forward shelter, and let subsequent ordinary stages decide. No panic reconnect is performed by this branch. | Evaluation waits. With the default switch off, there is no behavioral difference. | Regain the ability to answer attacks. | Stops travel even at full health, possibly for a character travelling precisely to obtain supplies or a weapon. `armed()` also has an intentional optimistic treatment of unread equipment, so this is not proof that all weapon failures will be caught. |

`fight_back` is a misleading name for the rate branch: this branch attempts a reconnect, not an attack. Similarly, `travelFleeFrom: players` does **not** prevent the current code from attempting a panic reconnect under monsters. It primarily changes whether the objective is abandoned. Setting `travelFleeFrom: never` is not a general “disable panic reconnect” switch either: the low-health branch still accepts `near.length` monsters, and the rate branch is separately controlled by `fight_back`.

The reconnect's consequence depends on location. [Autopilot.playDead](../tools/m59-autopilot.mjs) cancels outstanding movement before reconnecting. In the open it can enter a frozen interval, 90 seconds by default; the documented mechanic restores vigor without starting normal health recovery until an action sets the moved-since-entry flag. When it returns to a held safe spot it can turn there to enable healing instead. It refuses repeated unhelpful freezes in some cases. None of these outcomes should be described as a free, universally successful heal.

**Mechanisms shared by both, or absent from both.**

| Mechanism | What the current code does | Consequence for the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Health/position watchdog | Independent `watchdogTick` records observations while the pass awaits. | Shopping is not generally unobserved. A fresh health frame is not evidence that the restricted ladder acted on it. |
| Damaging travel wedge | With a recorded wedge taking hits, at least 4 seconds old by default, and health below the flee line: cancel, retain destination, request forward shelter. One rescue allowance per journey. | Present in both paths. Shopping already sacrifices continued movement in this condition; it does not categorically choose “keep going.” |
| Route refuge selection/rest | `goTravelling` installs `shelterPolicy`. Its default need threshold is below 95% health, or any damage in an outranking room; sanctuary/Underworld cases decline. The mover reaches a refuge and calls the shared rest handler. | Present in both paths when that movement implementation consumes the policy. This is a pause within the journey, not an abandonment. |
| Recorded-track refuge rest | After this repair, verified stations use the same need callback and shared arrival/rest handler, including full health, the restable vigor target, cancellation and damage handling. | No longer has a separate 50%/20-second rest policy. Fine track aims remain intact. Candidate stops are stations on the actual track, including its walked fallback; no new off-track detour is introduced. |
| Sanctuary rest and hop-wall recovery | `Autopilot.travel` supplies `onHop → travelHold` for both paths, subject to `holdBetweenRooms`, guard switches, eligibility and budgets. Successful track crossings already call the common hop hook. | Giving shopping another pass clock does not add these hooks; it already has them. They are themselves bounded and conditional. |
| Routine mid-hop wall cancellation | The large wall-rung commentary in `passTravelling` now surrounds bookkeeping and a spent-budget note; there is no corresponding active wall take-back branch there. | Running this ladder more often does not restore the historical “cancel on a scratch” behavior by itself. Restoring that removed behavior would be a separate policy change. |
| Kill a blocker / long attack episode | `clearPathCheck` and `fightBackCheck` return while `this.inert` exists, including a travelling hold. The remaining restricted ladder does not dispatch blocker combat. | Neither path receives these watchdog combat interventions while held for travel, despite `clear_path` being listed as allowed. A wider rewrite could accidentally add a fight and its delay; that is not the narrow scheduling difference tested here. |
| Freeze invalidation | Same existing checker in both paths; its movement/action coverage is outside this task. | No fix or expanded reliance is assumed here. |

Ordinary path shelter pauses and panic take-backs have different costs. A route refuge keeps the mover and its destination; after the bounded rest it continues the crossing. A take-back cancels the current movement generation, returns control to recovery, and requires a later resume. `resumeSuspendedJourney` can wait for a held wall, requested shelter, and recovery; it also applies retry/death/age limits. The default attempt ceiling is 12 and the default age window is 30 minutes, with additional recovery escape conditions. More take-backs can therefore mean more delays, replanning, and exhaustion of retry budgets even when each intervention looks reasonable alone.

**Why shopping may deserve a different decision.** Shopping is often a means of making future survival possible: ingredients enable food, food provides vigor beyond the resting cap, and weapons and sales solve problems that waiting cannot solve. A route that repeatedly interrupts before the counter can preserve immediate health while leaving the character unable to farm or buy what it needs. On the other hand, carrying purchase money or valuable cargo makes completing a doomed crossing an expensive choice. “Continue” and “stop” both spend something.

Current shopping code already makes some purpose-specific decisions in [continueTownTrip, ensurePurchaseFunds and poorShoppingRetryReady](../tools/m59-autopilot.mjs): it keeps the unfinished service cursor, verifies funding before purchase, preserves the bank prerequisite, favors an available nearby bank, and can defer unaffordable shopping so the assigned farmer can earn again. Its poor-supply mode uses a restable vigor floor of 70 by default. A timed reconsideration does not by itself justify abandoning an active return route to go shopping again; the on-route retry guard remains in place.

Those are explicit economic decisions. The missing pass dispatch is not: both travel entry paths receive the same guard defaults, and `passTravelling` does not select different thresholds from “buy herbs,” “deposit money,” or “return to farm.” If different journey purposes should take different risks, they should be expressed and observable as such. An accidental dependency on whether a caller awaits the movement is an unreliable way to preserve that distinction.

A future change also has to distinguish a travel leg from an in-progress bank or merchant operation. Cancelling a walk and interleaving a new action with a withdrawal, purchase reply, or inventory verification are different operations. Running the whole ordinary ladder concurrently with the shopping coroutine could create the second kind of interference. The comparison does not justify doing that.

**What previous failures tell us.** The original September 12–13 report recorded 74 deaths, predominantly in travel/recovery, with heavy commercial travel. It reproduced a timer undoing suspension, shopping continuing after a rescue, and frozen-state fallthrough/damage blindness. Those are evidence that handoffs can make an intended rescue harmful or ineffective. They do not establish the outcome of today's corrected handoffs against simply continuing the same trip.

The repository also records earlier wall take-backs at 75–85% health followed by no actual rest because the ordinary recovery threshold disagreed; repeated interruptions then produced idle exposure instead of healing. It records a route-arrival bug that rested characters beyond the intended refuge, and retreat branches reporting success even when retreat was disabled. These incidents support checking acquisition, recovery and onward progress rather than trusting the name of the intervention. They are historical examples, not an estimate of the current effect size. See [travel repair notes](travel-survival-2026-09-13.md), [keeper failure notes](m59-keeper.md), and the historical comments in `passTravelling`/`walkPivots`.

There is also evidence for the opposite risk: moving characters can lose health fast enough to die while position/stillness checks report progress. The current rate rescue was introduced to cover that case. Neither movement nor cancellation is a sufficient success metric. The useful outcome is reaching safety, recovering enough to continue, finishing the needed service, and returning to productive work without repeated exposure or death.

**Evidence needed before choosing a policy.** First measure the opportunities without adding actions: which decision would fire, current HP/vigor, recent damage, route progress, estimated remaining travel, next reachable refuge, and the shopping prerequisite/cursor. Then compare intervention outcomes in an isolated controlled setting, separating successful reconnects from failed ones, actual refuge arrival from selection, and completed purchases from arrivals at a shop. Record deaths and maximum-health loss alongside arrival time, health gained, time exposed, interrupted/resumed legs, supplies delivered, and subsequent farming. Count missed opportunities as well as interventions.

The present offline results support retaining the distinction until it is evaluated. They do not support a blanket claim that shopping would be safer with the extra reconnect decisions, or that it is safer without them. The shelter repair makes an already-authorized recovery mechanism consistent; the scheduling/policy question remains an explicit decision for the user after this report.
