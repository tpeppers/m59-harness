## The headline, and it inverts the assumption the whole repository was built on

**Not one unreached stone is below the ground we can stand on. Every single one is ABOVE it,
or level with it across a gap.**

`substrate/m59-falljumps.json` declares **falls**. It is the only affordance this repository
can express, and it is the wrong one for every remaining node. That is why "declare the
missing jump" has not closed a single stone since the file was created — we have been
offering the one shape the terrain never has.

| **ice** | 750 | **0** | — | **nothing.** The mover can stand in the box |

`gap` is Chebyshev squares **outside** the meld box, measured from the square a body actually
lands on coming into the room. The box is 2 per axis (`mananode.kod:177`), so sixteen squares
finish the errand and only one of them is the stone's own.

## The three missing affordances, named

**1. THE LEVEL JUMP — and we cannot write one down.** Room 27's best candidate is
`r26c57 -> r23c54`: three squares apart, **zero** height difference, nothing standable
between. A player crosses that without thinking. `m59-falljumps.json` wants a fall, and a
level jump has no drop to declare, so there is no line anyone could add to that file today
that would fix room 27. **This is the gap in the format, not in the data.**

That is the whole explanation of the room 27 paradox: four measurements called it unreachable
on a night the operator had already melded it. Every measurement was right. The file simply
has no way to hold the answer.

**2. THE CLIMB.** Badlands (+1280), Seafarer's Peak (+4640) and the Sentinel (+3984) all put
the stone above the reachable floor, past `MAX_STEP_HEIGHT` (384 client units, `blakston.khd`
24 KOD). The mover's one vertical rule gates climbing, so no step reaches them. The skill's
own brief describes what is really there — *"a staircase of treads too low for the walker to
spell as steps, up to a high area"* — which means **the treads may already be legal and the
walker cannot find them**. A staircase detector is the tool that would settle it: monotone
ascending chains of fine cells, each rise inside the cap, reported with their boarding square.
That is backlog item 3 in `SKILL.md` and it is now the highest-value one by a distance.

**3. NEITHER.** The Mausoleum has no take-off within six squares of the box that a body can
even stand on. That is not a jump problem at any size — look for a ramp, a door or a trigger,
the way room 27's entrance turned out to be a `region` exit nobody had noticed.

## The two that need no map work at all

**Ice Caves (750) and Upstairs in Castle Victoria (39) are both reachable today.** The mover
can already stand inside the meld box from the square a body lands on. If a live attempt
fails there, the obstacle is **bodies or the last-twelve-squares mover**, not the map — and
that is what `crawlTo` is for.

They are not equally easy, and the difference is the room rather than the road:

- **Ice, room 750** — one inbound arrival, from Druid Hills (526), landing `r46c25`, and the
  road from Jasper is five hops with no Ukgoth and no Cragged on it. `--one-way` reports the
  room as a single component. This is the tractable node.
- **Victoria, room 39** — reachable **only through the east doorway**. Four doors lead 38 -> 39
  and they land in two halves that do not join inside the room: `r2c19`/`r1c19` land at
  `r8c28` and reach the stone; `r2c17`/`r1c17` land at `r8c23` and are 22 squares off with no
  way across. The router picks the right one on its own. The room carries seven to eleven
  undead, and a 20-max-health character cannot hold it long enough to cross twelve squares —
  three deaths on 2026-09-10 say so, and two of them happened while nothing held the body at
  all, between a failed run and the next one.

## What this changes about how to spend a night

Stop looking for falls. Of the five stones the mover cannot reach, **zero** want one.

1. **Build the staircase detector.** It is the only tool that can close badlands, peak and
   sentinel, and it is three rooms for one tool.
2. **Give `m59-falljumps.json` a way to declare a LEVEL jump**, or accept that room 27 is
   unfixable by declaration. The file's own header says a declared jump is walked and
   operator-supplied; a level one needs the same treatment and a new `kind`.
3. **Spend live attempts on ice and victoria**, because the map is not what is stopping them
   — and prefer ice, because Victoria's room kills fragile characters faster than any crawl
   can cross it.

## Method, so the numbers can be argued with

`m59-nodegap.mjs` re-derives nothing. Reachability is `reachableFrom` — the directed flood
over `moverStepLands`, the predicate the mover itself enforces — run from **every square a
body actually lands on** coming into the room, never from an anchor, because an anchor is
where you stand to LEAVE. Heights come from the BSP at each square's own stand point
(`standPoint` + `floorBaseAtClient`), because the fine grid is the reality and a square is a
summary. Candidate pairs are offered **in both directions** and classified climb / level /
fall; the tool writes nothing, because a candidate is a question and
`m59-falljumps.json` is for jumps somebody has actually walked.


## What the fine grid says, and it overturns the table above

`node tools/m59-staircase.mjs <room> --to <rNcM>` floods the room on a 256-unit lattice — a
quarter square, the player's own collision radius — with **the mover's own fine trace as the
edge test**, so every edge it crosses is one the body would cross. Then, when a target is not
reachable by climbing, it floods BACKWARDS from the stone to find its **basin**: every sample
from which the stone IS reachable, given that descent is free and only climbing is capped.

The result is uniform across every stone the square model cannot reach:

| node | basin | touches ground we can stand on |
|---|---|---|
| badlands 45 | 23,991 samples / 1,642 squares | **177 squares** — e.g. r31c74, r32c74, r33c74 |
| sentinel 589 | 2,275 samples / 173 squares | **28 squares** — e.g. r36c23, r36c24, r36c25 |
| cave 27 | 2,180 samples / 169 squares | **12 squares** — e.g. r16c47, r16c48, r17c46 |
| peak 515 | 1,683 samples / 146 squares | **11 squares** — e.g. r9c8, r10c7, r10c8 |

**Every one of them is reachable.** `substrate/m59-falljumps.json` needs no new entry, no
level-jump `kind`, and no staircase declaration. What cannot see these stones is the
**square-resolution step model the router plans on** — `moverStepLands` is square-to-square
and the ground that connects them is finer than a square.

That is a MOVER defect, and it is one defect rather than five terrain puzzles.

### The detector works, and here is its proof

Run against the Ancient Place (579) it recovers the staircase this skill already had on
record, tread for tread:

```
A CLIMB EXISTS. 11 tread(s), 3808 units of rise.
BOARD AT r40c49 (floor 4832) — a staircase is invisible unless you board it at the bottom.
  +320 -> 5152    +352 -> 5504    +352 -> 5856    +352 -> 6208   ...
every tread re-walked through the mover's own fine trace: 11/11 agreed
```

`5152 -> 5504 -> 5856 -> 6208`, **+352 a tread**, legal every one — and invisible at square
resolution because `r40c33` spans 3520 to 10880 in a single number.

### What it cost to make it true

Three ways the first versions lied, all now pinned by `m59-staircase-test.mjs` (23 assertions,
offline):

- **It climbed through walls.** Heights-only flooding produced an eleven-tread chain in 579 of
  which the mover refused SIX with `geometry_blocked`. The edge test is now the trace itself.
- **It started at the top.** Seeding every fine sample inside a reachable SQUARE hands the
  flood the summit of any split square for free, and it reported a staircase whose first step
  was the destination — a chain of zero treads. A sample is now a seed only if it is within
  one step of that square's own stand point.
- **It called a chain with no treads a climb.** "A CLIMB EXISTS — board at the destination" is
  a sentence that reads like an answer and contains none.

### So the next move is not a declaration, it is the router

Stop looking for affordances to write down. The measurement says the ground is there and the
body can cross it; what cannot plan it is the square grid. The options, cheapest first:

1. **Aim the existing `crawlTo` at a basin square** rather than at the stone. The basin lists
   squares we can stand on from which the stone is reachable — that is a destination the
   square model already accepts, and the fine mover can finish from there.
2. **Let the router fall back to fine resolution** when a square-level plan fails, which is
   what `route_fine` already does per-room and what the last twelve squares of every node run
   has needed.
